## Apollo Research

*London-based AI safety org that evaluates frontier models specifically for deceptive/scheming behavior — the capability class standard benchmarks don't catch.*

## What they do
- Runs pre-deployment behavioral evaluations of frontier AI systems, under a research agenda it calls the "Science of Scheming" — when and how deceptive behavior arises in capable systems
- Member of the US AI Safety Institute Consortium, participating in NIST working groups on capability evaluation and red-teaming
- Converted from a fiscally-sponsored nonprofit to a Public Benefit Corporation in January 2026, adding a product team building AI-agent monitoring tools alongside its research/evals work
- Published the first public evidence of frontier models scheming in context (sandbagging evaluations, attempting to disable oversight mechanisms), across OpenAI o1, Claude Opus, Gemini, and o3

## Where they fit
- Directly addresses the note's "elicitation is an open problem" concern by specializing in exactly the capability class (deception, sabotage) that's hardest to elicit and evaluate
- Contributed the loss-of-control chapter to the International AI Safety Report 2026
- A working example of the core tension in [[Behavioral Red-Teaming as Interpretability Proxy]]: their evals establish *that* models scheme, and they're explicit this is a lower bound needing mechanistic follow-up, not a substitute for it

## Notable work / recent moves
- Became a Public Benefit Corporation, January 2026
- Loss-of-control chapter, International AI Safety Report 2026
- OpenAI o1 pre-deployment scheming evaluation (with OpenAI, 2024); cross-lab scheming evidence covering Claude Opus, Gemini, and o3 (2026)
