## CMU ICARM (Jeremy Avigad)

*Jeremy Avigad's group at Carnegie Mellon — one of Lean's original co-designers and a lead maintainer of mathlib, now directing the NSF-funded Institute for Computation and AI for Research in Mathematics (ICARM), sitting squarely at the intersection of interactive theorem proving and AI-driven formalization.*

## What they do
- Avigad co-authored "The Lean Mathematical Library" (mathlib community, CPP 2020), the paper describing mathlib's design — the single unified, community-maintained library that essentially all Lean formalization work (including AI-assisted provers) now builds on top of
- Directs ICARM, an NSF-funded institute explicitly organized around the intersection of interactive theorem proving, automated/neural reasoning, and mathematical research — a more AI-forward complement to human-driven formalization efforts
- Also works on automated reasoning and the philosophy/pedagogy of formal proof (teaching with Lean), not just library engineering

## Where they fit
- A useful complement to [[Xena Project (Imperial, Kevin Buzzard)]] inside [[Proof Assistants & Formal Mathematics]]: Xena is the highest-profile human-driven formalization effort (Fermat's Last Theorem), while CMU/ICARM is the academic anchor explicitly built around where AI and formal proof intersect
- mathlib itself is the shared infrastructure underneath both human-driven and AI-driven Lean work, including the training/eval corpora used by systems like AlphaProof

## Notable work / recent moves
- ICARM formally launched as an NSF-funded institute in 2026, positioned as a hub for "AI-driven mathematics" research at CMU
- Continues active maintenance and pedagogical work around Lean/mathlib alongside the institute's research agenda
