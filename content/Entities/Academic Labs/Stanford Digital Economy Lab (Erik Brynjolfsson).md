## Stanford Digital Economy Lab (Erik Brynjolfsson)

*Erik Brynjolfsson's Stanford lab, studying the actual measured economic and productivity effects of enterprise AI deployment — the empirical counterweight to vendor case studies.*

## What they do
- Economics research group measuring how AI tools change worker productivity, skill distribution, and labor markets in real deployments (not lab studies) — RCTs and large-scale field data, not self-reported vendor metrics
- Notably documents "skill-leveling effects": AI copilots often help novice/lower-performing workers far more than experts, sometimes with no gain (or a slight decline) for top performers

## Where they fit
- Directly answers this field's "what solutions feel fake" problem — vendor case studies that measure adoption instead of outcomes — by producing outcome-measured, peer-reviewed alternatives
- The skill-leveling finding complicates the "narrow vertical copilots always outperform" framing this note treats as inevitable: gains are uneven by user, not just by workflow fit

## Notable work / recent moves
- **Generative AI at Work** (Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond; NBER/QJE 2023) — study of ~5,000 customer support agents using an AI copilot: novice workers improved ~34%, top performers saw minimal gains or slight quality decline, the landmark empirical source for "skill-leveling" in enterprise AI
- Ongoing Stanford "Transformative AI" research agenda on productivity, labor markets, and policy as enterprise AI deployment scales through 2026
