## Baker Lab (UW Institute for Protein Design)

*David Baker's lab at the University of Washington — the academic lab behind RFdiffusion and the broader "de novo protein design" movement; Baker shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for this work.*

## What they do
- Designs entirely new proteins (not just predicting structures of existing ones) — binders, antibodies, enzymes, peptides — computationally, then validates them experimentally in-house
- Open-sources its design tools (RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN and successors) as the de facto academic standard other labs and companies build on

## Where they fit
- Directly answers this field's "structure prediction isn't the same as function/interaction prediction" problem — the lab's whole research program is generative design toward function, not passive structure prediction
- The clearest academic (non-pharma, non-Big-Tech) proof point that AI-designed biomolecules can clear real experimental validation, not just look plausible computationally

## Notable work / recent moves
- **De novo design of protein structure and function with RFdiffusion** (Watson, Juergens, Bennett, Trippe, ... Baker; Nature 2023) — the generative diffusion model for protein design that much of the field's "AlphaFold for design, not just prediction" framing now points back to
- 2026: designed antibodies entirely from scratch and computer-generated enzymes; a separate 2025 result reported designer proteins successfully binding 39 of 43 tested "undruggable" targets (91% hit rate) — concrete evidence against the "candidate, not drug" skepticism this field's notes flag elsewhere
