## MIT AI Policy Forum

*MIT Schwarzman College of Computing initiative, co-chaired by Aleksander Madry, Asu Ozdaglar, and Luis Videgaray — convenes government, industry, and academic researchers to translate AI policy principles into sector-specific implementation.*

## What they do
- Academic policy convening body (not a lone-PI lab) that runs task forces on specific sectors — mobility, finance, healthcare — each chaired by an MIT researcher and drawing in technical and policy experts globally
- Runs periodic AI Policy Forum Symposia bringing together regulators, companies, and academics to work through implementation-level questions rather than high-level principles
- Complements MIT's broader "AI Policy for the World Project" and the university's technical AI safety research (e.g. [[MadryLab (MIT)]]) with a dedicated policy-translation layer

## Where they fit
- Directly addresses this field's "what seems genuinely hard here" framing — writing rules for a capability curve that outpaces legislative timelines — by working the sector-specific implementation gap between principles and enforceable rules, distinct from [[CSET (Georgetown)]]'s empirical policy research or [[GovAI (Centre for the Governance of AI)]]'s compute/international-coordination focus
- One of the few academic (rather than think-tank or company) venues where regulators and frontier labs sit in the same room on implementation questions

## Notable work / recent moves
- Continues running sector-focused task forces and symposia through 2026 as the EU AI Act's full enforcement (August 2026) and NIST's agent-standards initiative create concrete implementation questions for the mobility/finance/healthcare sectors it already focuses on
