## Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security

*Biosecurity research center at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health — the academic public-health anchor for evaluating AI's bio-uplift risk, a domain most AI-native eval orgs aren't positioned to assess on their own.*

## What they do
- University-affiliated center (not an AI lab) applying decades of biosecurity/public-health expertise to the specific question of whether AI models meaningfully lower the barrier to biological weapons development
- Runs the AIxBio program, evaluating AI systems' potential to assist in bioweapons-relevant tasks and publishing governance recommendations grounded in public-health risk frameworks rather than ML benchmarking conventions
- Contributed to multi-institution biosecurity risk assessments alongside Stanford and Fordham researchers on how rapidly compute/data scaling could accelerate bio-uplift capability

## Where they fit
- Supplies the domain expertise this field's evaluators (METR, Apollo Research) generally lack — bio/cyber/persuasion uplift testing requires exactly the kind of specialized ground-truth judgment (what actually constitutes meaningful uplift toward a bioweapon) that a public-health security center, not an AI lab, is positioned to calibrate
- Directly relevant to this note's "absence of ground truth" problem: bio-uplift is the one dangerous-capability category with a real historical incident base (bioweapons programs, dual-use research of concern) to calibrate against, and JHU CHS is one of the few groups that actually holds that base

## Notable work / recent moves
- Co-authored the 2026 "Automated Laboratory Security Tiers" framework for evaluating and mitigating biosecurity risks from AI models' latent capabilities
- Participates in the global consortium of biological-model developers and evaluators committed to running dangerous-capability evaluations before model release
