## LBNL Energy Technologies Area

*Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's Energy Technologies Area — DOE national lab (operated by the University of California) that produces the reference data on U.S. datacenter energy demand.*

## What they do
- Publishes the "United States Data Center Energy Usage Report" series (Shehabi et al., first edition 2016, updated 2024/2025) — the report most cited by researchers, journalists, and policymakers for how much power U.S. datacenters actually consume and where it's headed
- Houses the DOE's Center of Expertise for Data Center Energy and runs the "AI for Cooling and Cooling for AI" program, working on liquid-cooling specifications adopted industry-wide (alongside Lawrence Livermore's Energy Efficiency HPC Working Group)
- Functions as the academic/national-lab counterweight to vendor-supplied efficiency claims — independent, government-funded measurement rather than industry self-reporting

## Where they fit
- The primary research-side (as opposed to vendor-side) source underpinning [[Datacenter Power & Cooling Constraints]] — when the field cites "rack power went from 30kW to 180kW" or U.S. datacenter electricity share, it traces back to LBNL's numbers

## Notable work / recent moves
- 2024/2025 update to the Data Center Energy Usage Report, mandated by the Energy Act of 2020, extending demand projections out to 2028 amid the AI buildout
- Active liquid-cooling specification work with industry (rack/cabinet cooling standards) as density climbs past air-cooling limits
