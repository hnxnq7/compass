## MIT Computer-Aided Programming Group (Armando Solar-Lezama)

*Armando Solar-Lezama's group at MIT CSAIL — arguably the modern origin point of program synthesis as an active research field.*

## What they do
- Develops new approaches to software synthesis that combine information from multiple sources (examples, partial specs, sketches) to produce the program a developer actually wants
- Originated Sketch, the "sketching" approach that set the template for subsequent synthesis-as-search approaches (Solar-Lezama's thesis work), and Rosette, a language for building solver-aided DSLs
- Broader agenda: reduce the skill and effort required to build software that's secure, reliable, and efficient, by automating the hardest parts of programming rather than just assisting with the easy parts

## Where they fit
- The clearest academic anchor for [[Program Synthesis]] specifically — Solar-Lezama won ACM SIGPLAN's Robin Milner Young Researcher Award for "reigniting the field," and Sketch/Rosette remain reference points for search-based and solver-aided synthesis
- Useful counterweight to the LLM-code-gen framing the field note is skeptical of: this group's whole thesis is search/solver-based correctness guarantees, not plausible-looking output
