## DiCarlo Lab (MIT)

*James DiCarlo's lab at MIT's McGovern Institute — the lab most associated with treating deep neural networks as quantitative models of the primate visual cortex.*

## What they do
- Established the "goal-driven deep learning" paradigm: train a neural network to perform a behaviorally relevant task well, then compare its internal representations to recorded neural activity, rather than fitting a model directly to neural data
- Runs Brain-Score, a standardized benchmark/leaderboard scoring how well artificial vision models predict primate visual cortex responses and behavior
- Combines large-scale electrophysiology in non-human primates with modern deep learning to test which computational principles are actually shared between brains and models

## Where they fit
- The academic anchor for the "does this model's internal geometry actually resemble a brain's, or is it just curve-fitting" question that [[Neuro-AI & Brain-Inspired Models]] is built around — complements Flatiron CCN's representational-geometry work with a task-driven, primate-electrophysiology-grounded approach

## Notable work / recent moves
- "Using goal-driven deep learning models to understand sensory cortex" (Yamins & DiCarlo, Nature Neuroscience 2016) — the field-defining paper establishing the goal-driven modeling paradigm now standard in NeuroAI
- Brain-Score (brain-score.org) remains an actively maintained public benchmark for vision-model/brain alignment
