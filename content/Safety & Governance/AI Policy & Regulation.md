## Problem Space: AI Policy & Regulation

## What seems genuinely hard here?
- Writing rules for a capability curve that outpaces the legislative/rulemaking timeline by years
- Reconciling genuinely divergent regulatory philosophies across jurisdictions (EU's binding comprehensive regime, US's more fragmented federal/state patchwork, China's algorithmic-specific rules, Singapore's agentic-AI-specific framework) for products that ship globally

## Why hasn't it been solved?
- Institutional / regulatory constraints — this is close to the whole problem statement: over 1,000 AI policy initiatives across 69 countries as of early 2026, with no real convergence on a shared framework
- Technical constraints — much of what regulation wants to require (e.g. reliable capability evaluation, provenance) depends on unsolved technical problems in [[Dangerous Capability Evaluation]] and [[Data Attribution & Provenance]]

## What solutions feel fake?
- Compliance checklists that satisfy the letter of a framework (e.g. NIST AI RMF) without any of the underlying technical oversight actually being robust

## What solutions feel inevitable?
- The EU AI Act (fully enforceable August 2, 2026) becoming the de facto global baseline the way GDPR did for privacy, since multinational companies build to the strictest applicable regime
- Continued regulatory attention shifting specifically toward autonomous agents (not just models) as the unit of concern, following NIST's 2026 agent-standards initiative
